# Automatic boarding and separate group drop-offs

Implemented on branch `codex/multi-destination-bookings`. Production activation is disabled by default. No migration rewrites existing bookings, rides, routes, historical records or presentation data.

## Passenger and driver behavior

The passenger app reads `/api/v1/pickup-requests/capabilities`. When it advertises flow version 2, the organizer books one shared pickup with a separate destination for each member. The first member is “You”; companions are “Passenger 2”, “Passenger 3”, and so on. Companions do not need accounts or phones. Each row selects a route stop or the drop-off pinned on the map. Multiple people can select the same destination. The booking dialog displays individual server-calculated fares and their combined total after price review. Editing destinations or group size invalidates that review. Every destination must lie on the route and after the pickup. The server checks available seats again when reserving the whole group.

Bookings are assigned to an eligible trip using the existing dispatch rules. Only version 2 trips accept new version 2 bookings. Roadside trips must be active and accepting requests; terminal bookings use the current loading jeep. Pickup pins display two distinct destination names and “+N more” above the pin. Tapping opens the member, destination, status and fare list. Passenger maps also show the individual destinations, including completed stops, and driver maps show remaining destinations. The ride continues for companions after the organizer gets off.

Driver acknowledgement, group-ready confirmation and driver boarding-count confirmation are absent from the version 2 flow. Booking notifications and assigned-jeep information remain. The Passenger app shows automatic drop-off status and the next remaining destination, ordered by the saved route stop order. It does not ask the organizer to choose who got off. If a stop order is unavailable, it omits the next-stop label rather than guessing. The assigned driver retains an optional manual unloading correction for missed GPS detection. It requires an idempotency reference and the current ride revision. Legacy passenger journeys retain their original manual completion control; legacy API compatibility remains available.

## API and records

`POST /api/v1/pickup-requests/quote` accepts the route, pickup type/location and member destinations. It returns normalized members, each fare snapshot, `partySize`, `regularTotal` and `quoteToken`. `POST /api/v1/pickup-requests` submits that token with `flowVersion: 2` and the member destinations. A changed price or destination returns HTTP 409 with `QUOTE_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and an updated quote; it does not create a booking. The passenger must review prices again before retrying. The app currently returns to booking review after this error, so previous companion selections may need to be entered again.

One pickup request and one ride session represent the group. Each embedded member has a stable ID within the booking, organizer flag, label, saved destination, fare snapshot, status, boarding time and completion time/method. Counters and total fare are derived from the member records. MongoDB transactions serialize reservation, boarding, cancellation, transfer, unloading and trip-ending writes. A unique pickup-request index prevents multiple rides for the same booking. Existing reservations count against capacity; completed, cancelled, expired and transferred reservations are not counted twice.

## Automatic boarding

Fresh organizer and assigned-jeep positions must stay within 25 metres for at least 10 seconds. Both GPS samples must be no older than 15 seconds and must advance beyond the start of the proximity window. The assigned jeep must have ordered location data. The trip, jeep and driver must remain eligible at commit time. A terminal trip must still be loading. A changed trip or GPS stream, missing/stale positions, or moving outside the radius resets the window. All reserved members board together, and their reservation converts into onboard occupancy in one transaction.

## Automatic unloading

Only accepted ordered driver GPS observations are used. A sample must be no older than 15 seconds, have valid coordinates and accuracy within 30 metres, and contain a known non-negative speed of at most 5 km/h. Missing speed is preserved as unknown, never converted to zero. Arrival requires at least three distinct observations covering at least 10 seconds within 50 metres of the nearest saved destination. Movement, poor accuracy, stale/invalid data, a new stream, a changed nearest stop or a gap longer than 15 seconds starts a new check. Duplicate or older observations cannot advance the arrival counter.

Completed destinations remain in the nearest-stop comparison. Remaining members at another nearby stop cannot inherit the completed stop’s dwell time. Only members assigned to the reached physical destination complete. Their seats release once; other members stay onboard. The ride closes when none remain. The organizer’s phone is not used for later unloading. Para alerts and approach notifications use member destinations. Reports group actual unloading events by their recorded destination and count. Ending a trip closes remaining members and records those seats at the jeep’s position as “Trip ended early”, rather than counting planned destinations as visited.

`ActivityLog` records `automatic_boarding`, `member_unloading`, `gps_boarding_rejected` and `gps_unloading_rejected`, including affected members, trip/ride references, released seats and rejection reasons. Boarding rejection logging records reason changes to avoid logging the same failure repeatedly. The booking’s `autoBoarding.lastReason` and ride’s `lastAutomationCheck` expose the latest diagnostic result.

## Compatibility and rollout

Set `AUTOMATIC_GROUP_FLOW_ENABLED=false` by default. `AUTOMATIC_GROUP_MINIMUM_BUILD` defaults to 21. When enabled, new driver journeys and passenger bookings require build 21 or newer. GPS check-in for a scheduled terminal journey also enforces this minimum. Existing active journeys continue to use their stored flow version and legacy completion endpoints remain available. Disabling the flag stops new version 2 bookings while version 2 journeys already in progress remain finishable. Do not rewrite flow versions on historical records.

Passenger and Driver release APKs are version 1.1.0, build 21. The published database maximum was build 20 for both apps at preparation time. The Admin web build is version 1.1.0. Builds use the existing signing configuration and production API URLs; they have not been published or deployed. For a physical staging run, rebuild both APKs with the isolated staging API URL (and staging-only fallback URLs), using `PARAGO_API_BASE_URL` and `PARAGO_API_BASE_URLS`. Do not use the production-targeted APKs to create staging fixtures.

Deploy the backend and worker together with the flag off. Test updated apps against a separately configured staging backend/database and verify real phones’ GPS, background tracking, notifications, terminal check-in, group drop-offs and reports. Publish both verified mobile releases before enabling the production flag and minimum build. Leave the legacy finish endpoints available. Cloud staging credentials are not configured in this workspace, so cloud staging and physical-device acceptance are still required before production activation.

Run it again with `BOOKING_FLOW_TEST_PROFILE=staging` to exercise production-mode configuration with deployment environment `staging`, synthetic accounts and a separate loopback replica set. These tests disable cloud integrations and never use the live database. Run `npm test` for backend regressions and `flutter test` in each mobile project for UI and client regressions.

The flow tests cover solo/group/shared/distinct destinations, different fares, organizer-first unloading, terminal/roadside boarding, seat limits, cancellation races, expiry, transfers, early trip ending, stale/inaccurate/missing-speed GPS, reconnects, drive-bys, nearby stops, duplicate/concurrent updates, manual corrections, failed notifications, authenticated GPS API wiring and legacy completion. Phone-width widget tests verify abbreviated map labels, complete fare lists and correction selection. Detailed results and installable build artifacts are in `artifacts/automatic_group_flow_2026-10-09`.

Automatic boarding assumes all booked members board with the organizer. Automatic unloading assumes they get off when the stopping rule is met. GPS cannot independently prove physical boarding or unloading; manual corrections remain available.

## Driver information and automatic trip lifecycle update

All active drivers assigned to a route can now read its pending pickup pins through `GET /api/v1/pickup-requests/route/:routeId`. The feed includes destinations, group size and the assigned jeep, with passenger account IDs and notes omitted. Other drivers cannot acknowledge, board or change the booking. One assigned jeep retains the seat reservation. Route subscribers receive a refresh signal after booking changes and boarding; private trip payloads remain in their authorized trip rooms. The Driver app coalesces refreshes and removes expired pins through its existing refresh loop.

Full trip automation requires both `AUTOMATIC_GROUP_FLOW_ENABLED=true` and `AUTOMATIC_DRIVER_LIFECYCLE_ENABLED=true`. The latter defaults to false. Updated app builds are still required for new journeys. An eligible assigned driver/jeep starts one version 2 trip after three fresh, ordered GPS observations over ten seconds, stopped within fifty metres of the configured departure terminal. The existing queue chooses the loading jeep and retains waiting jeeps. Only the loading jeep departs automatically after three moving observations over ten seconds outside the terminal radius plus fifty metres, within one hundred metres of the route and moving at least five km/h. Queue promotion and pending-booking transfer offers use the existing transaction rules.

At the last ordered route stop, an empty trip completes after its own three-observation, ten-second stopped arrival check. At least sixty seconds must have passed since departure/start; actual occupants, walk-ins and reservations must all be zero. A two-minute cooldown prevents an immediate new trip after completion. Freshness is fifteen seconds and maximum GPS accuracy error is thirty metres. Missing speed is unknown. Stale data, movement and changed streams reset checks; rejected or repeated ordered samples cannot advance them. Coding or queue restrictions block the automatic transition without rejecting an otherwise accepted GPS update. Activity and terminal-action logs record transitions and blocked checks.

The default Driver map provides information. Start/end/departure, walk-in adjustments and unloading corrections remain available as manual fallbacks. Backend capabilities control the automation label; when full automation is disabled, normal trip controls remain identifiable. Existing version 1 trips retain their original behavior, and no historical record is rewritten. Automatic completion does not end a trip containing passengers or reservations; intentional early endings remain manual.

Walk-in controls offer `-1`, `-3`, `-5`, `+1`, `+3` and `+5`. Each tap submits one count adjustment. Buttons are disabled when an adjustment would make walk-ins negative, exceed available capacity, or when the controls cannot accept a change. The two rows wrap on narrow screens or with enlarged text.

All user maps now share a centered jeep symbol that scales with a nominal road footprint at the current zoom. Before boarding, sustained passenger movement away from the waiting location or above fifteen km/h can show a `Still waiting?` card with `Waiting` and `Cancel`. Waiting retains the original pickup point; Cancel uses normal booking cancellation. This reminder does not identify another transport service or change boarding rules. See [marker and reminder behavior](ROAD_MARKERS_WAITING.md).
